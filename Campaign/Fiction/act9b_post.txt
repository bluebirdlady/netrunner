SATURATION

The Magistrate Revontulet Initiative was a broadcast license.

Not a programme, not a personality, not even a format — a license, pre-registered across forty-one Heinlein media markets, for a "long-form interactive narrative property" whose content fields were all marked "pending." The license had been purchased eleven months ago. It had been renewed twice since, at increasing cost, by a buyer GameNET's records listed only as "External — recurring."

You found the seventeen names from Earth Station's reassignment batch in a contract appendix attached to the license. Not as performers. As source material — biometric and behavioural profiles, licensed in perpetuity, for use in generating "synthetic participant likenesses" for the property once its content fields were filled.

GameNET was not making a show. GameNew was holding a slot open for one, and selling licensed copies of seventeen people into it, sight unseen, to a buyer that had been paying to keep the slot reserved for almost a year.

The "External — recurring" buyer's payment routing matched a pattern you recognised: the same six-hop anonymization signature from Mercury's relay, now appearing as a financial instrument instead of a data tap.

Mercury was not watching the market. Mercury was buying a position in it — and had been, patiently, for almost a year before you ever pulled the first thread at Kestrel-9.

You sent the license appendix to the same three channels as before, and turned to the second thread: AU Co., and whatever "Special Sourcing" had sold them.
