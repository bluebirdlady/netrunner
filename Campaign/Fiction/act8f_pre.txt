BUILT TO LAST
by Circuito Saudade

Weyland Consortium's position was straightforward: they were not doing anything wrong.

Their legal team had said this in a statement released four days after the documentation package began circulating. The statement acknowledged the existence of Project Midnight Sun as a legitimate infrastructure development initiative, described the autonomous zone framework as "a reasonable regulatory evolution for orbital development," and characterised the REGENESIS research as "a proprietary methodology within Jinteki's licensed operational scope." The false manifests were attributed to "administrative error" at a subcontractor level. The Senior Compliance Officer was released from the Pravdivost safehouse and described as a "contractor who is currently assisting with our internal review."

Twelve days to the vote. The council had not postponed it. Three members had called for a review. The other fourteen had issued no public position.

Weyland's project office for Midnight Sun was not on Heinlein. It was in Earth transfer orbit, at the facility the manifests had been feeding for six months. The facility that housed, per the Ob cargo records you had already pulled, 94 metric tons of processed rare-earth composite with classification OBX-CORVUS.

The facility that housed, per the HB development records, a pre-production unit of the CONTINUITY ENGINE.

Not a prototype. A production unit.

They had already built the first one.

—

TARGET: Weyland Consortium: Built to Last
FACILITY: Weyland Consortium Midnight Sun Project Office, Earth Transfer Orbit Station Kestrel-9
OBJECTIVE: Find the production unit. Document its operational status. Pull whatever Weyland has been using it for — because if it is already running, something has already been processed through it. Find what that is.
