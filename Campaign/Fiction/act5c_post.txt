WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

The archive connection lasted three minutes and fifty-one seconds.

---

H-982's facility logs documented four hundred and eighteen days of continuous operation. Personnel rosters, equipment maintenance records, environmental monitoring data for the deep core chambers. Safety incident reports — eleven of them, each filed and closed internally, none reported to any external body. The kind of paperwork that exists to demonstrate that paperwork was done.

The cohort records were in a separate partition. Designated by batch number, cross-referenced to AU Co. production runs and PT Untaian delivery manifests, timestamped from intake through the duration of each trial phase. Every entry in the same hand: precise, professional, the clinical notation of someone performing a function they had decided not to name.

The last entry was made six days before the facility's emergency decommissioning order.

You pull everything. You make it out with nine seconds to spare.

---

BANGUN's isolation perimeter sealed behind you. H-982's maintenance signal went silent an hour later — infrastructure removal phase, on schedule. The facility is gone now. The rock that's left doesn't remember what stood in it.

The record does.

---

You spend two hours assembling the final package. The Black Site files, the Zwicky transfer records, the PT Untaian research data, the AU Co. production manifests, the Editorial Division suppression registry with its authorization chain and Luana Campos's name on eleven pages, the Méliès U draft correspondence, and now the H-982 facility logs — all of it indexed, cross-referenced, formatted so that anyone who receives it can find the thread and follow it to the end.

You send it to every address you have left. To BONES, wherever they are. To the mirror network. To six newsies who went quiet and two who didn't.

You send it, and then you wait.

---

Some of it will be suppressed. Some mirrors will go dark. The name at the top of the authorization chain belongs to someone with the resources to make almost anything disappear, and they will try.

But the runner networks are fractal and paranoid and brilliant, and the data has been moving for weeks now, and the people who know how to read it have been reading it, and some of them have been talking to other people, and some of those people have been asking questions that can't be closed with a correction or a retraction.

The data exists. The record exists. H-982 existed — a specific place, a specific time, a specific number of people who made specific decisions and documented them carefully in a system that was waiting for someone to come back for it.

You got in. You got out. You have the record.

What happens next isn't a run.

It never was.
