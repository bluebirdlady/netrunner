BURN NOTICE: NOT SERVED
by Nneka Adewale-Mortimer

Classy Fire burned two assets in the first four turns. The selective destruction was precise — the assets targeted were the ones that would have allowed the Syndicate the most flexibility in the mid-engagement window, the credit-generating infrastructure that the defensive model depended on for sustained ICE maintenance. Ryo identified the vulnerability correctly and removed it.

The Syndicate's model adapted.

The executive meeting records were protected not through asset-based defense but through ICE depth — a configuration that does not rely on assets that can be burned. Deep ICE on the meeting archive server, stacked against a runner who is good at destroying things but still has to be good enough at breaking what remains to reach the core.

Ryo reached the outer layer of the meeting archive. Found three ICE subroutines and sufficient credit cost that the burned asset economy could not sustain the break. The run ended at the outer layer.

Two agendas were advanced in the window created by the asset loss — a counterintuitive move that the credit drain forced. The Syndicate accepted a defensive gap to create an advancement window. It held for two turns. It was enough.

Final score: Syndicate seven, runner three. Ryo scored through the burned-asset gap and through one outer server that was left deliberately under-defended to draw the engagement away from the archive.

The burn notice was not served. The records are secure.

—
Classy Fire burned what it found. It did not find the archive.
Elegance met depth. Depth held at the critical layer.
Executive records secure. The suite stands. The Syndicate endures.
