LAST SONG
by Nneka Adewale-Mortimer

Zahya sang, in the runner community's sense — committed fully, professionally, with every resource they had acquired across four months of engagements. The approach to the underground server node was the most complete display of Criminal methodology the monitoring team had observed: credential chains layered nine deep, hardware configuration optimized for this specific target, a credit reserve that suggested the contractor fee had been, in fact, substantial.

The liability ledger was behind an ICE stack that had been tripled in the forty-eight hours since the monitoring team located Zahya's contract.

Zahya burned through five layers. Six. Seven. The logs show a runner spending far beyond economic efficiency, past the point where the math said stop, past the point where a professional reassesses the engagement.

A hare song. The last beautiful thing.

The liability ledger moved to a secondary partition at layer four. What Zahya reached at layer eight was a copy — accurate through the 2051 fiscal year, stripped of all Project Crow references, sealed behind a fingerprinted access control that would tell the counterintelligence team exactly who accessed it, if anyone did.

Zahya did not access it. They reached the access control and stopped.

Then they left.

—
They made it further than anyone else.
The real ledger is elsewhere now, in a place only one person knows.
Zahya sang. The song ended. The Syndicate's books remain its own.
