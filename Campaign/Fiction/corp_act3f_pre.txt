ASSETS AND LIABILITIES
by Orla Vaszquez-Nkosi

Barry Baz Wong is Tri-Maf. That information, alone, changes the calculus of every decision the Syndicate's security directorate makes from this point forward.

The Tri-Maf is not an ideological movement. It is not a collective of angry anarchists or principled activists or mercenary extractors. It is organized crime at a scale that operates like a corporation, with hierarchy, with contracts, with institutional memory. Baz Wong is a professional in every sense of the word — and professionals take things personally when their employers take losses.

Someone hired Baz Wong. That means someone has been unhappy with the results of the earlier runner engagements and has escalated to a supplier that guarantees delivery.

The Syndicate Investment Division manages the Syndicate's internal financial architecture — credit flows, asset valuations, the ledger entries that track Project Crow's resource expenditure without naming it. It is, on the surface, exactly what it claims to be. The anomalies are buried deep.

"Professional Opportunities" — the current engagement's runner-board designation — suggests Baz Wong is treating this as routine business. Which means Baz Wong's client has made a credible offer. Which means the client knows enough about the Investment Division to believe the payout justifies the fee.

Protect the ledger. Score the financial agendas. Do not let Tri-Maf inside the division's books.

TARGET: Barry Baz Wong — Professional Opportunities
FACILITY: Syndicate Investment Division, Lunacent Financial Ring
OBJECTIVE: Defend financial records. Score investment agendas. Deny Tri-Maf access to Project Crow ledger entries.
