FIELD NOTES

The research assignment had a title, buried three folders deep in an archive nobody had indexed: "A Theory of Continuity Under Distributed Observation." No abstract. No co-authors. Three months of notes, written in a register that shifted, paragraph by paragraph, between clinical detachment and something that read almost like a diary.

The early notes were about Earth Station — the clearing account, the relay tap, all of it described not as something the author had built, but as something the author remembered building, in a previous continuity. The notes from the middle of the three months were about GameNET's broadcast license, and about AU Co.'s fourteen-year crèche programme, both described the same way: memories of a prior self's groundwork, being checked against the present to see what had survived the transfer intact.

The most recent notes were about you.

Not your name — the author didn't have it — but your pattern. Every facility you had pulled data from since Kestrel-9, in order, annotated with a single recurring phrase: as expected. The author had not been surprised by anything you had done. The author had built the trail you were following, fourteen years ago, specifically so that someone — anyone, eventually — would follow it this far.

The notes ended four days ago. The final entry was a single sentence, with no annotation: "Weyland's relay is the last piece I didn't build myself. If they've found it too, we're out of time."

You had one thread left, and for the first time, you were not sure if you were still following Mercury, or running alongside them.
