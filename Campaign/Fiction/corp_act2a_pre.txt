PARTY ANIMAL
by Desh Fuentes-Calloway

There is a certain type of runner that prefers chaos to precision. They don't pick the lock — they knock over the whole door and step through the wreckage, grinning.

Loup is that type.

The Syndicate Relay Hub sits above Lunacent's commercial district, routing traffic for forty-three corporate partners across the inner dome. It is not, on paper, a high-value target. It is a transit node. A switchboard. Its value is not in what it holds but in what passes through it — and Loup, apparently, has figured this out.

"Viral footprints on the outer relay," says the security digest at 0630. "Consistent with Anarch deployment patterns. Recommend elevated ICE posture on all transit nodes."

You pull up the Relay Hub's current architecture. The Anarch method is not elegant. Loup will seed the network with viruses — little programs that replicate, corrupt, degrade. They spread like rumors. They cost credits to clean. They cost time.

But Loup makes mistakes. Every Anarch does. They trust their chaos to do the work for them, and sometimes it does. But chaos has no loyalty.

The Hub's agenda stack is full. Three scoring windows in the next two operational cycles, if you move efficiently. Loup will be burning through their install queue. Let them burn.

Efficiency beats entropy. It always has.

TARGET: Loup — Party Animal
FACILITY: Syndicate Relay Hub, Lunacent Commercial District
OBJECTIVE: Score three agenda points before viral saturation compromises relay integrity.
