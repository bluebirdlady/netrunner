CASE CLOSED
by Temidayo Okonkwo-Bell

Topan's data fragments found the outer edge of the supply chain routing architecture and began building their mirror. It was elegant work — the kind of investigative tooling that, under different circumstances, the Syndicate's own counterintelligence division might have recruited for.

But the mirror image captured a server farm that had, at 0600 that morning, rerouted all Project Crow-adjacent traffic to a clean relay with no Syndicate designation attached. What Topan built a mirror of was a logistics node for four hundred and twelve bazaar vendors. Fruit importers. Textile distributors. An artisan collective out of Heinlein's east ring.

The logistics agendas scored cleanly at 0734 and 0801. Topan spent two more hours building a portrait of a server farm that sells mushrooms.

When the runner finally disconnected, their data file was complete and thoroughly useless.

In the post-incident debrief, Yemisi Arfewi allows herself a small smile. "Let them have the bazaar data," she says. "It's technically public anyway."

—
Topan came to build a case.
You handed them a case with nothing inside.
The supply chain moved on, invisible, clean.
