LIABILITY
by Nneka Adewale-Mortimer

Zahya is in Heinlein's criminal underground. The monitoring team tracked the trace to a physical location — a subterranean server node in a district where the Lunar Commerce Authority does not maintain regular oversight, where the lighting comes from borrowed power tap and the network runs on unregistered relays.

"Hare Song" — the runner board's designation for this engagement — is Zahya's signal to the community that this is a contract job with existential stakes. A hare song is, in the community's folklore, the last beautiful thing you make before you go quiet.

Whoever is paying Zahya for this final engagement has offered them enough credit to retire. Or enough to run one last time before they decide whether to retire. The monitoring team cannot determine which.

What Zahya is going after: the Syndicate's liability ledger. Not the clean version, the public-facing compliance documentation. The real one. The ledger that documents what the Syndicate actually did, what was paid for, what was covered up, and who authorized each decision. It exists in an encrypted partition on a server in Heinlein's criminal underground — which is, from a security perspective, deeply ironic — because the Syndicate's legal team determined that was the least likely place for a regulatory authority to serve a discovery order.

Zahya has, apparently, received intelligence about where "the least likely place" turned out to be.

—
TARGET: Zahya — Hare Song
FACILITY: Heinlein Criminal Underground, Unregistered Server Node
OBJECTIVE: Protect the liability ledger. Score final quarter agendas. Do not let Zahya leave with the real books.
