BRIEFING
by Circuito Saudade

Deputy Fernanda Gouveia received Epiphany Analytica's research briefings every Tuesday at nine o'clock.

They arrived in a clean format: twelve to fifteen pages, constituent polling data, policy analysis, predictive modelling on how each item on the current legislative calendar was likely to affect her reelection prospects in Belém do Pará. The analysis was always accurate. The recommendations were always framed as constituent representation. She had found, over the past eight months, that following the recommendations produced results that the polling data would subsequently validate.

She had found this reassuring.

She had also found, after five months, that she was reading the briefings in her private office rather than with her assistant Rodrigo present, which was different from her previous habit. She had told herself she was protecting Rodrigo from the volume of technical policy data. She had believed this for approximately three weeks.

The item on the current legislative calendar that had received the most briefing attention, across all fourteen months she had been a subscriber to Epiphany Analytica's data services, was the Automata Initiative. The briefings on this item were detailed, consistent, and always reached the same conclusion: the legislation was in her constituents' best interests. The first briefing had included a reference to a consulting services contract for R$400,000 that had appeared in her campaign finance filings, which she had not previously examined closely.

She had voted for the Initiative in committee.

She was scheduled to vote for it on the floor in forty-two hours.

She sat in her office and looked at the briefing on her screen and thought, for the first time in a long time, about what it would mean if the constituent polling data was not actually derived from her constituents.
