PEER REVIEW
by Temidayo Okonkwo-Bell

Dewi spent four hours in the R&D Campus's outer network and, according to the monitoring team, appeared to be having a wonderful time.

She found two legacy architecture remnants from a 2041 hardware upgrade that had never been fully decommissioned. She identified a subroutine interaction pattern in the Campus's third ICE layer that the Campus's own network team had not documented. She filed what appeared to be a bug report — a properly formatted one, with reproducible steps and suggested remediation — through a maintenance channel that should not have been accessible from her position.

The R&D agenda scored at hour two, in the gap between Dewi's second and third explorations of the legacy remnants. A second scored at hour three. The Campus's network team used Dewi's unauthorized bug report as the basis for a legitimate patch during the fourth hour.

Dewi never reached the Project Crow research nodes. The architecture she was navigating toward was, by hour two, behind a restructured firewall that presented as a routine data quarantine. She respected the quarantine. Didn't push through. Filed a note in her own access log: "Interesting. Come back."

The Campus network team archived her bug report. Remediated it. Kept it.

—
Dewi left the Campus more well-documented than she found it.
That is not what you wanted, but it is not what she came for either.
The Project Crow nodes remain sealed. For now.
