PROFESSIONAL COURTESY
by Orla Vaszquez-Nkosi

Baz Wong does not rush. The access logs for the Investment Division engagement show a runner who tested every ICE layer twice before committing to a breach — confirming costs, calculating credit windows, ensuring each move had a defined exit condition. Methodical. Patient. The polar opposite of Ryo Phoenix Ono's fury.

It made the defense cleaner, in some ways. A predictable approach is one you can anticipate.

The Project Crow ledger entries had been stripped from the Investment Division's active database at 2000 the previous evening, replaced with placeholder accounting records so generic they would survive an external audit. The financial agendas scored at 0830 and 0914 while Baz Wong was still working through the outer ICE structure.

The runner reached the inner server at 1020 and spent forty minutes in the financial records database. The Syndicate's counterintelligence team watched the access in real time and estimated that Baz Wong retrieved approximately 340 megabytes of accounting data before the firewall's ejection subroutine engaged.

340 megabytes of placeholder records. Zeros and ones arranged to say nothing at all.

Baz Wong's disconnect was clean. Professional.

Business, unfortunately, is not always what it appears.
