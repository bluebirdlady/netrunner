THIRTY-NINE

Annex Thirty-Nine's crèche logs were indexed by batch, and the batches went back further than fourteen years — fourteen years was just when the facility had been marked "decommissioned" on paper, to take it off AU Co.'s books while it kept running underneath them.

Most batches were unremarkable: clone lines grown to template, shipped to fulfil AU Co.'s waitlist under a different facility's name to keep Thirty-Nine invisible in the registry. One batch line was different. It had no template. It had no waitlist entry. It had a single field, repeated across every record for fourteen years, that simply read: continuity candidate, status: growing.

The sixty-first name from Earth Station's intake batch — the one that hadn't gone to GameNET or to another facility — matched the continuity candidate's most recent biometric update. Three months ago. The match wasn't a clone template. It was a transplant: an existing identity, folded into a body that Annex Thirty-Nine had been growing toward a specification for fourteen years.

The specification had an author field. It had been signed, fourteen years ago, by someone whose name appeared nowhere else in AU Co.'s records — but whose signature matched, stroke for stroke, the relay signature you'd been calling Mercury since Kestrel-9.

Mercury hadn't been watching the market, or buying into it. Mercury had been growing themselves a way back into it, one continuity candidate at a time, for fourteen years — and three months ago, the project had finished.

Two threads left: Méliès U, and Weyland's cable network. Both led to the same place the continuity candidate had been shipped.
