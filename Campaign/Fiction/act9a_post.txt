PROCESSING

The intake batch was sixty-one names long.

Each name had a contract status, a debt balance, and a field labelled "reassignment destination." Forty-four of the sixty-one were being reassigned to other Earth Station facilities. The remaining seventeen had a destination field that read only as a six-digit routing code.

You traced the routing code. It did not point to a facility. It pointed to a credit ledger — a clearing account that received a fixed transfer every time a worker's reassignment field was set to that code, and disbursed an identical amount, on the same day, to two recipient accounts. One recipient was a holding company registered to GameNET: Where Dreams are Real. The other was registered to AU Co.

The relay signature you had been tracking did not originate in Earth Station's systems. It was a passive tap — something reading the clearing account's transaction log every time it updated, and broadcasting a notification outward on the interval you had already mapped. Mercury was not running Earth Station. Mercury was watching the clearing account, the same way you were now watching it.

Whoever set up that tap had access deep enough to read transaction-level data from three corporations at once, without any of them noticing. That kind of access did not come from outside.

Seventeen names. Two destinations. You had two threads now, and for the first time, you were not the only one pulling on them.
