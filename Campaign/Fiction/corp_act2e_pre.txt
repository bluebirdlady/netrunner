FIELD TACTICS
by Nneka Adewale-Mortimer

Tao has been talking to someone.

The inference team at the Syndicate's counterintelligence division arrived at this conclusion after mapping the timing of the Research Station attack against a series of anonymous message drops on a runner-adjacent data board. The messages are encoded — nothing actionable — but the operational tempo matches. Tao is not improvising anymore. They're executing a plan developed with external input.

The Syndicate Comms Array processes encrypted transmissions for forty percent of Lunacent's corporate district. It is not a primary research or financial node — but it is where information travels. And Tao, it seems, has developed an interest in information in transit.

"Street Tricks," the runner community calls this approach: intercept the infrastructure layer, not the data layer. Harvest what passes through the wires rather than what sits in the vault. If Tao can establish a tap inside the Comms Array, they won't need to breach any of the Syndicate's primary servers. The data will walk through their fingers on its own.

The Array's ICE posture has been elevated. Traffic encryption has been reinforced at the packet level. But the Comms Array was not designed as a high-security node — it was designed to move data fast.

Fast and secure are not the same thing. You will need to build the gap closed before Tao finds it.
