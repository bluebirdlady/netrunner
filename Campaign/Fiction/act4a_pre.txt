PAPER TRAIL

Six days after the Black Site data started moving, someone began moving the money.

Not clumsily. Not visibly. The Zwicky Group doesn't make mistakes you can point to. They make the kind of mistakes that look like accounting conventions — rounding errors, inter-subsidiary transfers, credits routed through holding companies registered to addresses that exist for a quarter and dissolve. Forty-seven hops between the initial payment and anything resembling a name.

But it's there. Project Pale Horse had a budget. Someone signed off on every line of it.

---

You found the thread three days ago, buried in a partial file a newsie tried to verify before going quiet. A single transaction reference — Zwicky Group transfer node, Heinlein Belt — repeating across twelve months of Director Kase's internal correspondence. Always the same routing identifier. Always eleven days before a shipment authorization from H-982.

The amounts are too clean. People who are hiding money always make it too clean.

---

Tāo's workshop is still empty. You've stopped checking.

Your contact's server returned a response last night — one line, automated: ACCOUNT SUSPENDED. You don't know if that means they ran or if it means something worse. You've been careful not to think too hard about which is more likely.

The runner networks are still moving fragments of the data. Checksums match. People are reading it.

Someone at the Zwicky Group is watching that too.

---

You don't need to bring them down. You just need one thing: the account structure on the other end of those transfers. Something traceable enough that even a compromised newsie can't pretend not to see it.

The transfer node handles live routing. There will be a transaction log. There will be names.

You just have to get there before they finish cleaning it.
