SETTLEMENT
by Orla Vaszquez-Nkosi

Hiram 0mission's private server is meticulous. The monitoring team expected a spider's web — a tangled coordination architecture built by someone living in the runner community's informal infrastructure. Instead, they found something that looked like the work of a corporate security professional: clean, documented, structured, backed up.

It is, in retrospect, obvious. He was one.

Hiram entered the server at 0900, found the Syndicate's infiltration package immediately — it had not been particularly well-hidden, the team acknowledges — and spent the first twenty minutes doing nothing. Just assessing. Reading the infiltration's architecture the way a man might read someone else's handwriting on a wall of his own home.

Then he ran.

He was good. Twelve years of corporate security work and four months of runner experience in a campaign that had pushed the Syndicate's defenses further than any previous engagement. He knew how the Syndicate defended, because he had watched it defend, again and again, for four months.

But the server was already compromised, the agendas already positioned, the coordination documentation already moving to the Syndicate's evidence archive. What Hiram ran against was not a defender protecting a network. It was a vault that was being closed around him while he moved.

At 1043, the final agenda scored. At 1044, Hiram 0mission disconnected.
