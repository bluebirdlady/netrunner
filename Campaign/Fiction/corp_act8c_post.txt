STEEL AND SOIL: HELD
by Ivette Serrano-Park

The hardening process began at turn three. Steeling Keiko's resilience tools came online in sequence — not dramatically, but steadily, the way a material changes under heat rather than impact. By turn six, Esa's rig had achieved a state the Syndicate's monitoring team described as "approaching problematic." By turn eight, it was there.

The Syndicate had scored four agendas by turn six.

The strategy was sound: advance fast in the early window before the hardening completes, then defend. The problem with executing this strategy against Steeling Keiko is that the transition point is not clearly marked. The Syndicate's monitoring team identified it at turn six. Esa had begun acting on the hardened rig's capabilities at turn five.

One agenda was stolen in the gap between turn five and the Syndicate's recognition of the hardening threshold. The after-action team has flagged this for protocol revision.

The remaining agendas advanced cleanly. The coordination center's inner servers were not reached. Mining logistics contracts registered at 1156, ahead of the window when the hardened Steeling Keiko configuration could have breached the core.

Final score: seven to four. Esa's four came through sustained pressure in the late engagement, when the rig was fully operational and the ICE was under maximum stress.

The extraction contracts are intact. Steel from the asteroids continues to flow.
