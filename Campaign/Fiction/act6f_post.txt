COUNTER CULTURE

The routing code resolves to a single destination.

São Paulo. Brazil. Earth.

---

Forty-three nurseries. Eight hundred and twelve substrates carrying the Restoration Protocol at the cellular level. Six months of Thunderbolt hardware protecting the transit corridor. Twenty-two months of Weyland ice growing around the data envelope. Fourteen months of HB compliance architecture embedded into Jinteki production runs. Eleven months of distributed asset reports processed by people who never saw the whole.

And Nuvem SA's legal framework, filed in 114 pages, ensuring that no court in the solar system would have jurisdiction to ask what any of it was for.

You pulled everything.

The production schedule. The compliance embedding specifications. The deployment manifest. The routing code. The complete chain from the Pale Horse research to the Restoration Protocol to 312 units stamped for São Paulo.

---

Thirty-Nine's notebook page reached the external network six hours after you hit the facility.

She didn't know it would reach anyone. She put it where it could be found because the numbers didn't match and she had learned, in eight months of counting, that when the numbers didn't match it meant something was wrong, and when something was wrong you wrote it down.

That was the only rule she had made for herself.

It was enough.

---

The data has been moving for four days.

It moves the way the Pale Horse data moved — fragment by fragment, checksum by checksum, passed between people who know how to read when something real is coming through. But faster. The networks that carried Pale Horse have been waiting for the shape of the next piece. They recognise it immediately.

In Rio, in São Paulo, in the agricultural zones where the protest lines have held for eleven months, the numbers are arriving. 312 units. 97.4% compliance rate. A routing code that ends at their doorstep.

People who have been fighting Nuvem SA's law of the land now know what the law was for.

People who lost someone in the uprising know who built the architecture that was meant to make it permanent.

A bioroid in Annex Seven noticed an anomaly and left a buffer open.

Thirty-Nine kept a separate count.

You ran the servers.

None of you knew the others existed. None of you needed to.

What happens next isn't a run.

It never was.
