IRON CURTAIN
by Calvin Wong Tze Loon 黃子倫

Martins ran diagnostics at 06:00, same as every day.

Isaac Station Kilo sat at the L4 point, which meant it occupied the most gravitationally stable position in its stretch of the Belt, which meant someone who understood orbital mechanics had placed it here deliberately. Martins thought about this sometimes, on the long diagnostic cycles, when there was nothing to do but watch the numbers scroll. Choices had been made. Nothing about this station was accidental.

He thought about it the same way he thought about the ice.

The ice at Isaac Station Kilo was not commercial-grade. He could tell from the heat signature on the rez cycles, from the unusual power draw when the outer arrays initialised. He didn't have clearance for the Thunderbolt firmware specs, so he didn't know the specifics — but he had been maintaining this station for three years, and he knew what commercial ice looked like when it breathed. This wasn't it.

Every morning, before his diagnostics ran, the ice arrays registered a new advancement counter.

One. Per night. Every night. Automatic. Like a clock.

He had flagged this to his supervisor eighteen months ago. His supervisor had said: that's nominal. The Isaac Protocol runs continuous optimisation cycles. Martins had asked what the optimisation target was. His supervisor had said that Martins didn't need to know the parameters to maintain the hardware.

That was true. He ran the diagnostics. He logged the numbers. He moved on.

What he noticed, in the way you notice things you're not looking for, was that the ice wasn't getting harder to break. The advancement counters accumulated. The strength readings climbed. But the optimisation was directional — not outward, not against intrusion, but inward. Like a shell building walls around a very specific interior volume.

He had written this observation in his personal log eighteen months ago, and then deleted it.

Outside the hull, two hundred and twelve kilometres of vacuum and the quiet of the outer Belt. L4, precisely where you'd want to be if you were building something you needed to last.

He ran the diagnostics. The numbers came back. He logged them.

Then, as he had done every morning for the past four weeks, he left the terminal on and went to make coffee, and did not think about what he had noticed.
