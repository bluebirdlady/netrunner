ORBITAL MECHANICS
by Temidayo Okonkwo-Bell

The coalition's coordination was evident in the approach: Topan was not running blind. They came in with pre-mapped ICE configurations — data that could only have come from the Melies City Annex engagement, cross-referenced against BANGUN's public telemetry signature. The coalition's collective dataset was, genuinely, impressive.

It was also six weeks old.

BANGUN's network architecture had been rotated completely in the previous thirty days. The ICE configurations Topan's coalition had mapped were gone, replaced with a new generation of subroutines designed by a team that had never worked on a Syndicate project before — contractors from an unrelated firm, hired for this specific rotation.

Topan's pre-mapped approach hit walls that didn't exist in their data. The three-second communication lag compounded the problem — by the time their rig adapted to what it was actually encountering, the ICE had already processed the access attempt and queued a response.

Platform agendas scored at 0612 and 0701. Topan disconnected at 0744, the coalition's collective map thoroughly invalidated.

The coalition's data board went quiet for fourteen hours after the engagement. When it came back online, it contained a new entry: a call for fresh reconnaissance.

—
They came with a map to a place that had already changed.
BANGUN holds its position above the equator, indifferent to their preparations.
The coalition's first attempt on the project ends in silence.
