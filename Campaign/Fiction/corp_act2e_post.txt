SIGNAL INTEGRITY: MAINTAINED
by Nneka Adewale-Mortimer

The tap attempt came at 0214. Tao had been watching the Array's traffic patterns for six hours prior, building a timing model precise enough to identify a seventeen-second window in the encryption cycle where packet-level interception was theoretically viable.

Theoretically.

The window had been closed at 2300 the previous evening, during a standard maintenance cycle that was, on this occasion, not standard at all. The Syndicate's counterintelligence division had received the inference team's report at 1800. By 2300, the gap was gone.

Tao hit a sealed wall at 0214 and spent the next forty minutes trying to find another way in. The comms-related agendas scored at 0251 and 0309. By 0340, the Array had processed ninety thousand packets of corporate traffic without a single interception event.

The anonymous message drops on the runner board went quiet after that. Whoever had been feeding Tao the tactical analysis had, apparently, run out of useful things to say.

—
The wire held.
Whatever Tao was supposed to find in the Array's traffic — it wasn't there.
The signal passed clean, sealed, untouchable, as it always should have been.
