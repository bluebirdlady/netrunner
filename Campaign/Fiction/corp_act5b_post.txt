BELOW THE HORIZON: SECURED
by Ivette Serrano-Park

The lateral approach arrived as predicted. Lat installed three tools in the first two minutes — a pace that the Syndicate's monitoring team characterized afterward as "alarming and efficient." The efficiency was real. The alarm was noted.

What the lateral approach requires, in order to function, is time. Enough draws. Enough credits. Enough room to build before the walls go up around the thing you're trying to reach.

The Syndicate did not give them time.

Two agendas advanced in the opening window, behind ICE that had been stacked specifically for depth rather than strength — not to stop a single hard push but to slow the drip-drip-drip of repeated efficient access. Lat is not a bulldozer. They are water. The facility had been waterproofed.

Lat ran the research lab twice. The first run made it to the second layer before the ICE held. The second run did not make it to the first layer. The engine was running, efficient and elegant, but the engine was running out of credits faster than the draw could replenish.

The Syndicate scored its seventh point at 1229.

Lat disconnected at 1231. The research data is intact. The transit hub research lab is back below the horizon where it belongs, invisible in the facility listings, earning its anonymity.

Speed answered the lateral thinker.
The engine was elegant. We were faster.
Research intact. Horizons maintained.
