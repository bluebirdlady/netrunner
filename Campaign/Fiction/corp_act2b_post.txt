EXPENSE REPORT
by Ivette Serrano-Park

Zahya ran the numbers twice. The second pass was slower — more careful, the kind of recalculation you do when the first result surprises you.

The outer ICE cost them more than projected. The secondary layer cost them more still. By the time Zahya reached the third server, the rig's memory suite was running hot, the credit account was bleeding, and the agenda inside was still two advancement counters from scoring window.

They didn't quit. That much the logs make clear. Zahya is not the kind of operator who quits. But they ran out of runway.

The IP filings moved to the Lunar Commerce Authority at 1400, three hours after Zahya's final disconnect. Registered, timestamped, protected.

In the incident debrief, Security Coordinator Pilar Ostergaard notes that the runner showed "considerable tactical sophistication" and recommends a review of contractor credential issuance protocols.

She is not wrong. The gap was real. It has since been closed.
