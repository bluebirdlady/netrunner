SPECIFICATION

The prototype existed. You found it in a clean room on the facility's fourth sub-level, surrounded by thermal management hardware and a faraday cage that would have been extreme for any commercial development environment.

HB called it the CONTINUITY ENGINE.

The development records described it as a "high-density cognitive substrate processor with reversible identity-architecture storage." The specifications it was built to — the margins HB had engineered into every component — matched Jinteki's REGENESIS parameters exactly. Cross-reference timestamps showed that the REGENESIS research cycles and the CONTINUITY ENGINE development had been coordinated from the beginning.

The commission chain was in a project management file that someone had not finished deleting. Eighteen months of coordination between HB Precision Design, Jinteki Research Division, and a Weyland Consortium infrastructure planning group. All of it routed through a Pravdivost compliance framework. All of it timestamped before the first false Ob manifest.

Project Midnight Sun had been planned for two years before the Automata Initiative passed.

The vote had established the legal foundation they needed. The autonomous zone would provide the operational freedom. The CONTINUITY ENGINE would run at scale in a space where no one could audit it.

Fourteen days to the vote.

You sent the full documentation package — cargo metadata, compliance records, campaign assets, REGENESIS files, CONTINUITY ENGINE specs, commission chain — to the analyst, to three council members, to Mercury's contact address, and to every journalist whose contact information you had ever touched.

Then you found the last thread. It ran back to the beginning.

To Weyland.
