ASH AND SIGNAL
by Temidayo Okonkwo-Bell

Ryo's name appears in the Syndicate's incident registry for the fourth time this quarter. The registry entry has a flag on it — a small orange marker that the intelligence division uses to indicate a runner who has crossed a significance threshold. Three flags total in the active roster. Ryo holds one of them.

The deck called Fizzy Phoenix is an optimization rig: everything tuned for efficiency, nothing wasted, the whole assembly running lighter and faster than the Syndicate's ICE profiles expect. Ryo does not overwhelm. Ryo threads.

The Syndicate's signal relay station above the Lunacent transit dome handles communications routing for eleven orbital platforms. If a runner can get into the relay's archive core, they can listen to seven months of encrypted Syndicate traffic — not decrypt it, not in real time, but the data exists, and data that exists can eventually be cracked by someone with the right tools and the right time.

Ryo has tools. Ryo is patient.

Your supervisor's briefing is concise: "The relay stays dark. If Ryo gets into the archive core, we have a problem that doesn't end when the run ends."

You understand. This is not about protecting one facility. This is about preventing a future problem from becoming a present catastrophe.

Ash and signal. The relay hums above the dome, translating light into language, language into light.

Keep the signal controlled.

—
TARGET: Ryo — Fizzy Phoenix
FACILITY: Syndicate Signal Relay, Lunacent Transit Dome, High Antenna Array
OBJECTIVE: Deny archive core access. Protect communications routing. Advance relay-dependent agendas.
