KAKURENBO PHD
by Circuito Saudade

The building did not have a name. It had a sub-level designation — Sub-Level Seven of the Méliès U Doctoral Archive — and an access record consisting of a single entry, fourteen years old, that read: archive sealed, contents pending continuity review.

"Continuity review" had taken fourteen years. You suspected it was about to finish.

The archive's environmental systems were running at full capacity, the way Annex Thirty-Nine's had been, the way TR-19's repeater had been: a piece of infrastructure built fourteen years ago, kept alive at full cost, waiting for a single moment. Every thread you had pulled — Earth Station, GameNET, AU Co., Weyland's trunk — had been a piece of the same waiting. Mercury hadn't been building a network to use it gradually. Mercury had been building a network to use it once, completely, the moment the continuity candidate was ready and Sub-Level Seven opened.

Three months ago, the candidate was ready. Four days ago, Weyland's security division read the routing table for the network that would open this door. You did not know if that was a coincidence, or the thing that had finally started the clock.

Either way, the door was about to open. You intended to be on the other side of it when it did — kakurenbo, the diary's author might have called it. Hide and seek. Except this time, you were the one who knew where to look.
