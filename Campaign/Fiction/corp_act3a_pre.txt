SCAVENGER
by Temidayo Okonkwo-Bell

The AU Co. Orbital Platform drifts at L2, a repurposed cargo hub that the Syndicate acquired eighteen months ago through an intermediary holding company. On paper it belongs to a logistics firm with no Haas-Bioroid affiliations. In practice, it houses the physical infrastructure for Project Crow's orbital relay network — antenna arrays, data routing hardware, power conditioning systems that exist nowhere in the Syndicate's official asset registry.

Loup's Devourer is not a virus. It is a category.

The threat assessment team spent four hours trying to classify it and ultimately concluded: Devourer is a suite of linked viral payloads designed to consume not just data but architecture. It does not steal. It digests. Networking nodes, ICE subroutines, asset management protocols — Devourer is built to eat the structure itself and leave nothing.

Loup found the Orbital Platform. The question is how. The shell company chain should have held for another year. Someone is helping them navigate it.

Three other runners have now been linked through cross-referenced incident logs. This is not individual enterprise anymore. This is coordination.

But you still have defenders. You still have ICE. You still have the Platform's agenda stack, which is moving to secure transfer the moment the windows open.

Whatever Loup is trying to devour, protect it before the first bite lands.

TARGET: Loup — Devourer
FACILITY: AU Co. Orbital Platform, Earth-Moon L2
OBJECTIVE: Defend orbital relay infrastructure. Score before viral saturation. Deny architecture access.
