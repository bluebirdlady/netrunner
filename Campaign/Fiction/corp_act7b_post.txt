LUCKY STRIKE: ABSORBED
by Orla Vaszquez-Nkosi

The lucky strike came on the second run. Baz ran server three with insufficient credits to break the outer ICE according to any conventional calculation, and then a credit event fired in sequence — a chain that the Syndicate's probability models assigned a fifteen percent chance of occurring in that precise window — and the outer ICE was broken anyway. Server three was accessed. The agenda inside was stolen.

Baz scored three points in one run. The Syndicate's after-action team noted this in the report with the flat prose of people who have seen lucky runs before and know that noting them is the only appropriate response.

It did not decide the engagement.

The distribution infrastructure agendas had been advanced in the first window, before the high-variance play materialized. The Syndicate had scored five points before Baz's luck event. When the lucky strike landed, the count was five to zero. When it resolved, the count was five to three.

The Syndicate scored its seventh point at 1203. Baz scored once more after the lucky run, bringing the final count to seven to four.

Seven to four. The margin is comfortable. The fuel distribution center is operational. The probability models will be updated to reflect the fifteen-percent event, which has now occurred and therefore can be planned around.

Luck that has been spent once is easier to prepare for.

Lucky Bravado found one strike. It landed. It was not enough.
The distribution network holds. The fuel moves.
