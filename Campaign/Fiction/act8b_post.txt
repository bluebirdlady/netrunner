ADVANCEMENT

The compliance records for Project Midnight Sun occupied four encrypted partitions and a shredding queue that had been running continuously for eleven days.

You got there before the queue finished.

Project Midnight Sun was not a construction programme. It was a framework — a legal architecture assembled over eighteen months by Pravdivost on behalf of a consortium of clients including Weyland, HB, Jinteki, and NBN. The framework's purpose: to establish regulatory precedent for a new category of orbital infrastructure.

The category did not have an official name yet. Internally, Pravdivost called it "autonomous zone designation." In practical terms, it meant a region of Heinlein's orbital space that would operate under a corporate charter rather than a council mandate — no bioroid labour regulations, no audit requirements, no independent safety inspections.

The framework was almost complete. Three regulatory approvals remaining, all of them accelerating through council chambers on a schedule that Pravdivost had managed very carefully.

The Senior Compliance Officer who had signed the Ob manifests was not missing. She was in a Pravdivost safehouse on Heinlein Station 2, under what the internal records called "client protection custody." She had found the same things you found, seven weeks earlier, and had told someone she trusted.

The framework went live in thirty-one days.

You send the compliance records to the analyst who first found the manifests. Then you find the next thread.

It runs to NBN.
