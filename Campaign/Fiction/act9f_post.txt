MERCURY

Continuity review completed while you were still inside.

Sub-Level Seven held one thing: a single occupied recovery pod, fourteen years of life-support telemetry scrolling past on a wall of displays, and a release sequence that had just finished its countdown. The pod opened. The continuity candidate — the body Annex Thirty-Nine had spent fourteen years growing, the body the diary's author had been transferred into three months ago — sat up, looked at the displays, and then looked at you.

They did not seem surprised. as expected, the diary had said, every time, about everything you had done. They reached for a terminal at the edge of the pod bay and pulled up a single file: a complete record of the network you had spent two acts dismantling, annotated not as a confession, but as a resignation letter. Every node, every relay, every fourteen-year wait — all of it had been insurance, the diary's author explained without being asked, against a version of themselves that no longer existed. The version that had built Project Midnight Sun's first continuity engine, the one Weyland had been running at Kestrel-9. The version that this body had been grown to replace.

"I built the network so someone would find it before they did," they said, nodding at a second file on the terminal — Weyland's security division, the routing-table read, four days old. "I didn't expect it to be you. But it's better that it was."

They handed you the terminal. On it: the full architecture of the continuity engine, the location of every other one Weyland had built since, and a name — their own, the one from before the transfer, the one that had signed the fourteen-year-old specification at Annex Thirty-Nine.

You had spent two acts looking for Mercury. Mercury had spent fourteen years looking for you — or someone like you — and had just handed you everything they had.

Somewhere above Sub-Level Seven, Weyland's security division was still deciding what to do about TR-19. They were about to find out it no longer mattered. The continuity engines were still running. There were more of them than anyone outside this room knew about.

You had a new target list, and for the first time since Kestrel-9, you weren't following a trail someone else had laid down fourteen years ago.

You were laying down your own.
