THE LEDGER

The briefing database held fourteen months of correspondence, payment authorisations, and outcome projections.

Ninety-four deputies. Eleven senators. Three state governors. The Federal Attorney General. The database was organised by Epiphany Analytica's standard client framework, which meant each subscriber received personalised content derived from their specific pressure points: reelection prospects, personal finances, family situations, and in seven cases, information about previous conduct that Epiphany Analytica had determined would be damaging if it became public.

The payment structure ran through six jurisdictions. Total: R$43.7 million, disbursed across eighteen months in tranches sized below the automatic audit threshold.

They had bought the Automata Initiative piece by piece, vote by vote, briefing by briefing. The constituent polling data in Deputy Gouveia's briefings was real polling data — it had just been conducted in different constituencies from hers, selected to produce the outcomes Epiphany Analytica needed. Results, delivered.

The database also held one item that wasn't a client file. A communications record, flagged for deletion and not yet purged: a single exchange between an Epiphany Analytica data analyst and an account that resolved to a Mercury routing identifier. The data analyst had been passing timing information — when the vote was scheduled, which delegates were soft commitments — to someone on the outside.

Another door left open. Another person who knew that when the numbers didn't match, you wrote them down.

The ledger was in four networks within twelve hours of the extraction. Deputy Gouveia's office released a statement that evening requesting a thirty-day postponement of the floor vote pending an independent review of the legislative process.

She was the first.

She was not the last.
