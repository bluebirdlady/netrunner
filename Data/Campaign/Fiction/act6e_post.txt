DISTRIBUTED

The node was never labelled. It didn't need to be.

You found it the way you find any hidden centre: by following the flows. Eleven feeders, each reporting to one address. The address resolving to a server with no public record, buried in the Heinlein agricultural belt's maintenance subnet, drawing power from a grid allocation registered to a defunct soil-remediation company.

Inside: eleven months of aggregated production data. And all of Yetunde's forwarded pieces, and all the other pieces from all the other nodes, assembled for the first time into a complete picture.

The picture is a production schedule.

The substrates are alive. The asset reports are health metrics. The agricultural cover is a clone nursery — forty-three facilities, none individually large enough to trigger the licensing thresholds that require Jinteki to register them, all of them collectively large enough to produce what AU Co. is scheduled to receive in eleven days.

Forty-three facilities. Eleven months. The Restoration Protocol embedded at the cellular level, generation by generation, until the compliance rate is 97.4%.

The clones at the end of this pipeline will be compliant in ways that clones have never been compliant before.

And AU Co. has been waiting for them.

The routing address for final delivery resolves to a single destination: AU Co. Orbital Production Unit 1, Heinlein L2 Lagrange Point.

You have found the production line.

Now you find what it feeds.
