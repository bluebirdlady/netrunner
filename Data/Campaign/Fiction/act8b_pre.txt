PRESSURE TEST
by eniteris

The incident report for Platform Gamma-7 lists three cause-of-death entries: decompression sickness, equipment failure, and acute cardiac event. Each is filed separately. Each is signed by a different Jinteki physician. Each is dated the same week.

The platform sits on the surface, but its data runs deep. The operation logs descend in real time from the drilling head — depth readings, pressure telemetry, drill-string torque — and all of it routes through a secure partition that Thule calls the Pressure Archive. They use the same word for the data and the environment it describes. You do not think this is an accident.

You have spent the last six hours mapping the platform's architecture from the relay data you pulled. The security model is layered the same way the ocean is layered: warm and accessible near the surface, cold and lethal at depth. The upper partitions handle routine operations — crew manifests, logistics, surface communications. Below that, the drilling data. Below that, something the partition labels only as SUBLEVEL.

The crew who logged the SUBLEVEL entries were the three who died.

You are running this from a floating transit hub two hundred kilometres away. The connection is low-latency and the maintenance window is twelve hours out. You have time to be careful.

You have read the depth readings. You know what pressure does at six kilometres. You know what it does to equipment. You know what it does to people.

You pull up the drilling logs and start counting downward.

—

TARGET: Thule Subsea: Safety Below
FACILITY: Extraction Platform Gamma-7, Atlantic Deep Water
OBJECTIVE: Access the Pressure Archive. Retrieve the SUBLEVEL data and the full drilling telemetry.
