The seventh tier is a signal archive.

Not data about a signal — recordings of a signal. Wideband acoustic captures, beginning at a depth of five kilometres and increasing in complexity as the drill head descended. The final recordings, taken at 5,847 metres, are not geological noise. They are structured. Repeating. The spectral analysis in the archive uses the word "non-random" with the careful neutrality of someone who has considered every alternative and rejected them.

The SPECIMEN LOG is worse.

The three crew members from Platform Gamma-7 spent their final working week at this station. The log records their neurological assessments before, during, and after exposure to the signal recordings played through a modified acoustic array. The before readings are normal. The during readings are not. The after readings are absent, because the physicians stopped recording after the second day and the crew members were transferred to the surface, where they died within a week.

Dr. Keeling's notes conclude with a recommendation: the acoustic exposure protocol should be revised. The note is dated the day before the deaths. It is the last entry she filed from the Annex.

Her subsequent filings are from a different location — an orbital station in the Atlantic coverage zone, registered to Issuaq Adaptics. The classification code on her transfer order is DEEP WATER.

The seventh tier also contains a project summary. It was written for a non-technical audience — for lawyers, you think. For people who needed to understand what the project was without understanding what it meant.

What it meant, translated out of the language of legal filings and research abstracts, was this: they found something at the bottom of the Atlantic. They do not know what it is. They intend to reach it.

The borehole is already drilling.
